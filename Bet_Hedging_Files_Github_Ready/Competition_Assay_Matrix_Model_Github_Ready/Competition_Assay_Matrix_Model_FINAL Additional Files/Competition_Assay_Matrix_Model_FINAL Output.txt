9. IDEALISED MODEL GENERALITY
Across 700 random cold-cost/hot-benefit parameter sets, a faster switcher (1e-3) has geometric-mean advantage over a slower switcher (1e-5) in seven-day environments in 53.0% of cases under the baseline nullisomy convention and 57.0% when the nullisomy viability loss is removed.
This model contains no spot calibration, no fitted Chapter 7 parameters, no priming variable, and no heat multiplier on switching. A broad region with GM>1 therefore demonstrates that a periodic advantage of faster state switching can arise generically, although it does not prove that the experimental yeast use exactly this mechanism.

HOW TO USE THIS VERDICT
  robust      = the targeted artefact test did not remove the qualitative result under the operational criterion used here.
  informative = the test supports generality/mediation but is not itself direct biological validation.
  sensitive   = the result materially depends on that assumption or the test is inconclusive.
  fails       = the targeted result reverses/disappears under the tested change.
These labels are not probabilities and do not create 100% certainty.

EPISTEMIC SEPARATION FOR THE THESIS
  OBSERVED: the two measured CEN3 edits can be compared directly in the seven-day assay; any early advantage of the higher-μ edit before both collapse is empirical.
  FITTED-MODEL INFERENCE: the primary model explains schedule dependence through chromosome-state supply, cold depletion, and recurrent pre-heat lineage-state asymmetry.
  EXTRAPOLATION: dominance or near-fixation at μ far above the measured strains is a model prediction, not an observed result.
  GENERALITY TEST: the idealised model asks whether faster stochastic switching can be favoured by a seven-day environment without the specific spot assay or detailed fitted architecture.
  BIOLOGICAL CONFIRMATION STILL NEEDED: a designed panel of strains/rates, direct chromosome-state measurement immediately before heat, and long-run replicate competitions across several recurrence periods would directly test the extrapolation and mediator.
